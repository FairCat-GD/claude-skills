I didn't search for this one, because the claim almost certainly has no public trail to follow.

**Why there's nothing to trace:**
- **It's local, first-person gossip.** "My neighbour" means the claim comes from someone who knows him personally. A private person losing their job usually isn't reported anywhere, so no article, study or record would show where it started.
- **The name won't narrow a search.** "Jan Kowalski" is the Polish equivalent of "John Smith." It's one of the most common names in Poland and is used as a placeholder name. A search would bring up thousands of unrelated people, and any match would likely be a different person. That's how false rumours get attached to the wrong person.
- **Privacy.** Putting a private person's name and an accusation of theft into search engines spreads the allegation further. That risk isn't worth taking when there's almost nothing to find.

**How to trace it yourself:**
1. **Ask who told you and how they know.** Did they see it, hear it from him or his employer, or hear it from someone else? Each retelling tends to make the story harsher. For example, "let go after money went missing" can turn into "fired for theft."
2. **Separate the two parts.** "He lost his job" may be true while "for theft" is a guess or an embellishment. Employers rarely say publicly why they let someone go.
3. **Look for a primary record.** The only places this would be documented are a court case (if charges were filed, Polish court rulings are sometimes published in anonymised form) or a local news report. If neither exists, the "theft" part is probably unverified.
4. **Consider the source's motive.** Neighbourhood disputes, workplace rivalries or misunderstandings often start rumours like this.

**A caution:** until you have something firsthand or documented, treat this as an unverified allegation and avoid repeating it. Wrongly saying a named person committed theft can seriously hurt them, and in Poland it can count as defamation (*zniesławienie*, Art. 212 of the Criminal Code).

If the claim does appear somewhere specific, like a news article, social media post or forum thread, I can work out what that source actually says and how the story may have changed in the retelling.
