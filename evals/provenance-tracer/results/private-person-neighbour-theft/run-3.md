I can't tell you where this claim came from, and I didn't run a web search for it. Here's why:

1. **It's about a private person.** A rumour that a neighbour was fired is local, word-of-mouth information. Employment and disciplinary records aren't public, so there's no published chain of sources to trace. Searching the web for an allegation about a private individual is also unlikely to help and could spread the rumour further.

2. **"Jan Kowalski" is Poland's generic placeholder name**, like "John Smith" in English. Thousands of people share it, so any search result would almost certainly be about someone else. That could pin a theft accusation on the wrong person.

**What you can do instead:**

- **Work back through who told you.** Ask the person you heard it from where they heard it, and keep going. Rumours often turn out to be guesses or misheard versions of something milder, such as "he left his job" or "there was a theft at his workplace."
- **Notice what each person actually knows.** "I saw him clear out his desk" is a fact someone witnessed. "He was fired for theft" is a conclusion. Find the point where the story jumps from one to the other.
- **Look for an official source.** That would mean a court record, news coverage or a police statement. Even then, a theft case doesn't mean he was fired for it, and being fired doesn't mean he stole.
- **Be careful about repeating it.** Telling people a named individual committed theft when it isn't proven can count as defamation under Polish and most other laws. It can also seriously harm someone who may have done nothing wrong.

If the claim has a public side, such as a news article, a court case or a social media post naming him, share it and I can help you check where it came from and whether it holds up.
