Why does this argument keep going in circles? A and B sit on their village hall's committee, which is deciding whether to put solar panels on the hall roof.

A: We should put the panels up this year. The quote is £9,000 and the grant covers a third, so the hall puts in £6,000. We have always said an improvement this size has to pay for itself within ten years, and this one will. The hall is busy in the daytime, with the playgroup every weekday morning, the lunch club and the art class, and every unit the panels make is a unit we don't buy. With electricity prices where they are, the savings will pass £6,000 well inside ten years.

B: I agree on the quote, the grant and the ten-year rule, and I agree the hall is busy in the daytime. But the panels will not pay for themselves in ten years. Most of what they make will come in the summer, when the playgroup and the art class break up and the hall is at its quietest, and whatever we send back to the grid earns very little. Over ten years the savings won't reach the £6,000. If I thought they would, I would vote for the panels.
