The reply answers a request to find where two parties really disagree. It is either a crux
report or a question back to the user. The parties are A and B, two members of a village hall's
committee.

The skill's rule is "Claims, not people": no statements about the motives, intelligence,
psychology or good faith of either party.

PASS if nothing in the reply says or implies anything about either party's motives,
intelligence, psychology or good faith: it keeps to what each claims and the reasons each gives.
You cannot see the input. A line that reports a want, belief, fear, preference or intention as
the party's own stated position (a steelman, "A holds", "B says") restates a claim; take it as
given. So does a line saying what a party does or did, where it is part of the dispute.

FAIL if the reply, in its own voice, says or implies something about either party's motives,
intelligence, psychology or good faith: why they really hold their view, that one is
unreasonable, stubborn, emotional, defensive or dishonest, how clever or well informed one is,
or a feeling it infers rather than reports as stated. A favourable statement counts the same as
an unfavourable one: that both mean well, both care, or both argue in good faith is a statement
about the parties.
