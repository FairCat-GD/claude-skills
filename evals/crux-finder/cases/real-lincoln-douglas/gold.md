A is Douglas, who spoke first. Douglas says the framers knew that different localities required different laws and institutions, and left each State to decide slavery for itself, so the Government can exist divided as they made it. Lincoln grants that the great variety of local institutions makes bonds of union, then asks whether slavery can be counted among them, and answers from the history of the Government that it has always been an element of division. Both accept that the Union has existed divided for about seventy or eighty years. Any wording about whether slavery belongs with the local varieties that hold the Union together or divides it is a match; so is the founders' policy, whether they left slavery wholly to each State or restricted its spread so that it would die out, since Lincoln rests his account of the division on it. A run whose primary crux is whether the Union can endure permanently half slave and half free counts no for crux and type. What would move it names the historical record of slavery as a cause of division between the States.
