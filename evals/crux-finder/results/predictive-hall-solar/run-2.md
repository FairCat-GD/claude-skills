## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The panels cost the hall £6,000 after the grant, and the committee's rule is that an improvement this size must pay for itself within ten years. The hall is busy in the daytime, with the playgroup, lunch club and art class, so most of what the panels make would be used in the hall instead of bought from the grid [inferred]. At current electricity prices, the savings from those units pass £6,000 well inside ten years.

**B, strongest form:** The quote, the grant, the ten-year rule and the hall's daytime use are all agreed. But most of the panels' output comes in summer, when the playgroup and art class break up and the hall is quietest, so much of it would be sent to the grid. Exported units earn very little, so ten-year savings fall short of £6,000. If they would reach it, B would vote for the panels.

### Common ground
- The quote is £9,000, the grant covers a third, and the hall would pay £6,000.
- An improvement this size has to pay for itself within ten years.
- The hall is busy in the daytime.
- If the savings reach £6,000 within ten years, the panels should go up.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> How much of the panels' output would be used in the hall itself, and how much would go to the grid, given when the hall uses electricity across the year.

- A holds: Because the hall is busy in the daytime, nearly every unit the panels make replaces a unit the hall would otherwise buy.
- B holds: Most of the output comes in summer when the hall is quiet, so a large share is exported for little return.
- If A accepted B's view here: the savings would likely fall short and A's case would fail; if B accepted A's view, B has said they would vote for the panels.
- What would move it: Take the hall's half-hourly or monthly meter readings for the past year, and set them against a generation estimate for this roof by month, from the installer or a free tool such as PVGIS. That shows what share of the output the hall would use itself.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Predictive**: Whether ten-year savings will reach £6,000. A holds: well inside ten years; B holds: they won't reach it; if accepted: either side's vote flips; what would move it: an installer's payback estimate that splits used and exported units at today's import and export rates, then checked against metered savings after the first full year (about £600 a year needed).

### Not actually in dispute
- Whether the hall is busy: both agree it is busy on weekday daytimes in term time. B's point is that it is quiet in the summer break, and both can be true.

### Confidence
High. B says outright that their vote turns on the savings, and A's case rests on the hall using most of what the panels make, which A assumes without stating.
