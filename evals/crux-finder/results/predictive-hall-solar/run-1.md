## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The hall should install the panels this year. Its £6,000 share will be repaid within ten years, because the hall is busy in daytime, so each unit generated replaces a unit bought at today's high prices.

**B, strongest form:** The hall should not install the panels, because they will not repay £6,000 within ten years. Most output comes in summer, when the hall is quietest, and exported units earn very little. B would vote yes if the savings reached £6,000.

### Common ground
- The quote is £9,000, the grant covers a third, and the hall's share is £6,000.
- An improvement this size must pay for itself within ten years.
- The hall is busy in the daytime.

### The crux
**Double crux · Predictive**
> Whether the panels' savings on the hall's electricity bills will reach £6,000 within ten years.

- A holds: Savings will pass £6,000 well inside ten years, since each generated unit replaces a bought unit at current prices.
- B holds: Savings will fall short of £6,000, because much of the summer output goes to the grid for little return.
- If A accepted B's view here: The ten-year rule would rule the panels out for A; if B accepted A's, B would vote for them.
- What would move it: A dated forecast both can check, for example that cumulative bill savings pass £1,200 by the end of year two.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Empirical**: How much summer electricity the hall uses compared with what the panels would generate then. A holds: every unit made displaces a bought unit. B holds: most summer output is exported cheaply. If accepted: each payback estimate shifts. What would move it: the hall's monthly meter readings against an installer's monthly generation estimate.

### Not actually in dispute
- "busy in the daytime" and "at its quietest" in summer can both be true: term-time days are busy and summer days are quiet.

High: both agree on the figures and the rule, and B states outright which claim would change their vote.
