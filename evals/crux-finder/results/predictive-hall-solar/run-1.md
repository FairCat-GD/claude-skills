## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The hall should put the panels up this year because they meet the committee's ten-year payback rule on the £6,000 net cost. The hall is busy in the daytime, so it will use most of what the panels make itself [inferred], and each of those units is one it doesn't have to buy at today's high prices. On that basis the savings pass £6,000 well within ten years.

**B, strongest form:** The panels should not go up because they fail the ten-year rule the committee agrees on. Most of their output comes in summer, when the playgroup and art class break up and the hall is quietest, so much of it will go to the grid instead of being used in the hall. Exported units earn very little, so ten-year savings fall short of £6,000. If they did not, B would vote yes.

### Common ground
- The quote is £9,000, the grant covers a third, and the hall pays £6,000.
- An improvement this size must pay for itself within ten years.
- The hall is busy in the daytime during term.
- Units sent back to the grid earn far less than units used in the hall save.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the hall uses electricity during the summer months, when the panels produce the most, in amounts close to what the panels would generate then.

- A holds: Daytime use is high enough that nearly every unit the panels make replaces a unit the hall would otherwise buy.
- B holds: In summer the hall is at its quietest, so a large share of the output would be exported at a low rate.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's savings estimate would fall and could miss the £6,000; if B accepted A's, B's shortfall would disappear and, by B's own statement, B would vote for the panels.
- What would move it: Set the hall's past 12 months of meter readings or bills, month by month and preferably by time of day, against the installer's monthly generation estimate, and calculate how much would be used on site and how much exported.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Predictive**: Whether the panels' savings will reach £6,000 within ten years. A holds: yes, well inside. B holds: no. If accepted: either side's vote would flip. What would move it: Before voting, agree a ten-year savings forecast built from the usage match above at current tariffs, and check it against the bills each year after installation, starting with a first-year target of at least £600.

### Not actually in dispute
- Whether the hall is busy in the daytime: both agree it is in term time. A is talking about term-time weekdays and B about the summer break, and both can be true. That is why the argument goes in circles: each side keeps restating a fact the other already accepts.

### Confidence
High. Both parties say the decision turns on the ten-year savings, and the only thing they disagree about underneath it is how much of the summer output the hall would use itself.
