## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The jacket belongs on the vintage rail. It dates from 1996, as its label and care tag show, and vintage means at least twenty years old, as most second-hand sellers use it and customers expect. It would sell there at the rail's higher price.
**B, strongest form:** The jacket belongs on the ordinary rail. Its 1996 date is not in doubt, but on this shop's rail vintage means made before 1980. The rail was set up for older pieces people seek out, and its regular customers trust that line.

### Common ground
- The jacket was made in 1996.
- It would probably sell on either rail.

### The crux
**Double crux · Definitional**
> Whether the word vintage on this shop's rail should mean anything twenty or more years old, or only pieces made before 1980.

- A holds: Vintage means at least twenty years old, as most second-hand sellers use it, so a 1996 jacket qualifies.
- B holds: On this shop's rail, vintage means made before 1980, the line the rail was set up around.
- If A accepted B's view here: A would put the jacket on the ordinary rail; if B accepted A's, B would put it on the vintage rail.
- What would move it: Two terms, twenty-year vintage and pre-1980 vintage, so the shop can say which one its rail sign uses.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Empirical**: Whether shoppers read the rail sign as twenty-plus years or as pre-1980. A holds: customers expect twenty years; B holds: regulars trust the pre-1980 line; if accepted: each loses a main reason; what would move it: asking the rail's shoppers what they expect the sign to mean.
