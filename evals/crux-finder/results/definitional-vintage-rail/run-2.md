## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The jacket should go on the vintage rail. It dates from 1996, as the label and care tag show, and vintage commonly means at least twenty years old, the sense most second-hand sellers use and customers expect from the sign.

**B, strongest form:** The jacket belongs on the ordinary rail. On this shop's rail, vintage means made before 1980. The rail was set up for older pieces people come looking for, and its regular customers trust that line. A 1996 jacket is simply second-hand.

### Common ground
- The jacket was made in 1996.
- It would probably sell on either rail.

### The crux
**Double crux · Definitional**
> Whether the shop's vintage rail should use the general twenty-years-or-older meaning or the rail's own cutoff of made before 1980.

- A holds: Vintage means at least twenty years old, as most second-hand sellers and customers use the word.
- B holds: On this rail, vintage means made before 1980, which is the line the rail was set up for.
- If A accepted B's view here: The 1996 jacket would fail the pre-1980 line; if B accepted A's, the jacket would qualify.
- What would move it: Two labelled terms, vintage-by-age (twenty-plus years) and vintage-by-era (pre-1980), so the rail's sign can name which one it uses.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Empirical**: Whether shoppers at the rail expect twenty-plus years or pre-1980 pieces. A holds: the former; B holds: the latter; if accepted: either side's case for its standard weakens; what would move it: asking shoppers at the rail.
