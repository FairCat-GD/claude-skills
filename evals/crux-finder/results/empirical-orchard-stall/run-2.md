## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** Week by week, last season's takings book shows that once each Saturday's pitch fee is deducted, the stall lost money. The orchard needs every pound for new trees and the irrigation repair, so a stall that cannot cover its pitch costs money the orchard does not have.
**B, strongest form:** The bank statements for the same season show the stall came out ahead after every pitch fee, if narrowly. The orchard needs every pound for trees and irrigation, and a losing stall should go, but this one doesn't lose; selling juice directly gets a better price than other buyers pay.

### Common ground
- The orchard needs every pound for the new trees and the irrigation repair.
- If the stall doesn't cover its pitch fees, it should be dropped.
- The right test is last season's stall income minus the pitch fees.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the stall's income last season was more or less than the pitch fees it paid.

- A holds: The takings book, checked week by week, shows the stall took in less than its pitch fees, so it lost money.
- B holds: The bank statements for the same season show the stall took in more than its pitch fees, so it came out ahead, though narrowly.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's stated reason for dropping the stall would go, since A objects only to a stall that can't cover its pitch; if B accepted A's, B would drop it, as B said.
- What would move it: Reconcile the takings book with the bank statements Saturday by Saturday, matching each recorded day's takings to its deposits and each pitch fee to its payment, to find which record leaves something out or counts it twice.
