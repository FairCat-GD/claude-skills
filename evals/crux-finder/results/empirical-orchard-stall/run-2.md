## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The orchard should stop taking a stall. A week-by-week check of last season's takings book shows that, after the pitch fee for every Saturday attended, the stall lost money. With every pound needed for the new trees and the irrigation repair, a stall that cannot cover its pitch is spending money the orchard does not have, however pleasant it is.

**B, strongest form:** The orchard should keep the stall. A check of the same season's bank statements shows that, after every pitch fee, the stall came out slightly ahead. B shares the priority on trees and irrigation and would drop a stall that lost money. This one does not, and selling the juice directly earns a better price than any other buyer pays.

### Common ground
- The orchard needs every pound for the new trees and the irrigation repair.
- The stall should be dropped if it loses money after pitch fees, and kept if it does not [inferred for A].
- The question is about last season, with all pitch fees deducted.
- It is nice to see people buying the juice, but that does not decide the matter.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the stall's income last season was more or less than the pitch fees it paid.

- A holds: The takings book, read week by week, shows the stall's income fell short of its pitch fees.
- B holds: The bank statements show the stall's income exceeded its pitch fees, though only by a small amount.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's case for dropping the stall would lose its basis, since A's objection is that the stall cannot cover its pitch; if B accepted A's view, B has said they would drop the stall tomorrow.
- What would move it: Line up the takings book and the bank statements Saturday by Saturday for last season and settle each difference. Check for cash takings that were never banked or were banked late, card payments that went into the bank but not into the book, pitch fees for Saturdays the stall did not trade, and any costs one record includes and the other leaves out. Then agree one net figure.

### Secondary cruxes
None.

### Not actually in dispute
- What the orchard's money should go on: both put the trees and the irrigation first.
- Whether the stall is worth keeping at a loss: neither says it is.
- B's point about a better price: A does not dispute that selling directly pays more per bottle. It only matters if the stall's net result is in question, and it comes back to the same reconciliation.

### Confidence
High. Both parties name the same test, profit after pitch fees, and say plainly what result would change their conclusion. Their only stated difference is which record of the same season they read.
