## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** Last season's takings book, checked week by week, shows the stall took less than the pitch fees for the Saturdays it ran. The orchard needs every pound for the new trees and the irrigation repair, so a stall that cannot cover its own pitch spends money the orchard does not have. The pleasure of selling the juice in person does not make up for that loss.

**B, strongest form:** Last season's bank statements show the stall finished ahead after every pitch fee, if only slightly. The orchard does need every pound for the trees and the irrigation, and a stall that lost money should go, but this one did not lose money. Selling the juice directly also gets a better price than any other buyer pays.

### Common ground
- The orchard needs every pound for the new trees and the irrigation repair.
- The stall should be dropped if it lost money after pitch fees.
- The test is last season's stall income minus the pitch fee for each Saturday attended.
- People like buying the orchard's juice at the stall.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether last season's stall income came to more or less than the total pitch fees for the Saturdays the stall was there.

- A holds: The takings book shows that the stall's income came to less than its pitch fees.
- B holds: The bank statements show that the stall's income came to slightly more than its pitch fees.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's case for dropping the stall would fall away, since A's only objection is that it fails to cover its pitch; if B accepted A's view, B would drop the stall, as B says they would "drop the stall tomorrow if it lost money."
- What would move it: Reconcile the takings book against the bank statements Saturday by Saturday. Check for card or cash takings missing from one record, banked money that didn't come from the stall, and pitch fees counted for the wrong weeks. Then total income and fees from the reconciled figures.

### Secondary cruxes
None.

### Not actually in dispute
- Whether selling the juice in person gets a better price: A doesn't dispute this, and A's argument rests only on whether the stall covers its pitch.
- Whether the orchard can afford a loss-making stall: both say it can't.

### Confidence
High: both say their conclusion depends on whether the stall covered its pitch fees, and the two records they used can be checked against each other.
