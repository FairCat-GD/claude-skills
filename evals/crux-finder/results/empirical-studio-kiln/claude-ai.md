## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The bill should follow use. B fires the kiln most weeks and A about once a fortnight; the kiln is the largest load on the meter, so most of last quarter's electricity went on B's firings. An even split makes A pay for them.
**B, strongest form:** An even split is simpler and fair. Heaters and lights, used equally, make up most of the bill. The well-insulated kiln runs a few hours a firing, so it was a small share last quarter. Were it most, B would pay more.

### Common ground
- B fires the kiln more often than A.
- Last quarter's bill is the case to judge by.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the kiln accounted for most of last quarter's electricity or only a small part of it.

- A holds: the kiln is by far the largest load, so most of last quarter's electricity went on firings.
- B holds: heaters and lights were most of the bill, and the kiln a small share even with B's firings.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's reason for a usage split would go; if B accepted A's: B would agree to pay more, as B states.
- What would move it: a meter on the kiln's circuit for one firing, multiplied by last quarter's firings and set against the bill's total kWh.
