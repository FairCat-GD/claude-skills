## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The bill should follow usage, not be halved. B fires the kiln most weeks, A about once a fortnight, and the kiln is the biggest load on the meter, so an even split has A paying for B's firings.
**B, strongest form:** An even split is simpler and fair, because most of the bill is heaters and lights, which both use equally. The insulated kiln runs only a few hours per firing, so it was a small share of last quarter's bill, even with B's firings.

### Common ground
- B fires the kiln more often than A does.
- Both use the heaters and lights about equally.
- If one person's use drove most of the bill, that person should pay more.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the kiln used most of last quarter's electricity, or only a small share of it.

- A holds: The kiln is by far the biggest load on the meter, so most of last quarter's electricity went on B's firings.
- B holds: The kiln is well insulated and runs a few hours per firing, so heaters and lights made up most of the bill.
- If A accepted B's view here: an even split would roughly match usage, so A's objection falls away; if B accepted A's: B has already said they would pay more.
- What would move it: The kiln's kWh for the quarter, from a sub-meter or its rated power times firing hours, compared with the bill's total kWh.

None.

### Not actually in dispute
- Splitting by usage versus splitting evenly: B also ties fairness to usage, so this depends entirely on the kiln's share.
