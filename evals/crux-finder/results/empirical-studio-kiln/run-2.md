## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The bill should follow usage, not be halved. The kiln is by far the biggest load on the meter, and B fires it most weeks against A's once a fortnight, so most of last quarter's electricity went on B's firings.

**B, strongest form:** An even split is simpler and fair, because most of the bill is heaters and lights, which both use equally. The kiln is well insulated and runs only a few hours per firing, so it was a small share of last quarter's electricity, even with B's firings.

### Common ground
- B fires the kiln more often than A does.
- If one potter's equipment used most of the bill, that potter should pay more.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the kiln used most of last quarter's electricity or only a small share of it.

- A holds: The kiln is the biggest load on the meter, so B's frequent firings used most of last quarter's electricity.
- B holds: The insulated kiln runs only a few hours per firing, so the heaters and lights, used equally, made up most of the bill.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's case that an even split makes A pay for B's kiln would fall away; if B accepted A's, B would pay more.
- What would move it: The kiln's kWh for last quarter, from its power rating and firing log or a plug-in meter, compared with the bill's total kWh.
