## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** Each potter should pay for the electricity they cause. B fires the kiln most weeks while A fires it about once a fortnight, and the kiln is by far the biggest load on the meter, so most of last quarter's bill came from B's firings. An even split therefore has A paying for B's kiln use.

**B, strongest form:** An even split is simpler and also fair, because most of the bill is heaters and lights, which both use equally. The kiln is well insulated and runs only a few hours per firing, so it was a small share of last quarter's electricity, even with B's firings. Had it used most of the bill, B would agree to pay more.

### Common ground
- B fires the kiln more often than A.
- Both use the heaters and lights about equally.
- If the kiln made up most of the bill, the person firing it more should pay more. A argues this directly, and B grants it outright.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> What share of last quarter's electricity went on the kiln, compared with the heaters and lights.

- A holds: The kiln was by far the biggest load, so most of last quarter's electricity went on B's firings.
- B holds: The kiln is well insulated and runs only a few hours a firing, so it was a small share of the bill even with B's firings.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's case for a usage-based split loses its basis, because the shared heaters and lights would make up most of the bill and a usage split would come out close to even anyway; if B accepted A's view, B has already said they would agree to pay more.
- What would move it: Multiply the kiln's rated power (kW, on its data plate) by the firing hours logged last quarter and compare that with the total kWh on the bill. Better still, put a sub-meter or energy monitor on the kiln for the next few firings.

### Secondary cruxes
None.

### Not actually in dispute
- **Whether to split by use or down the middle.** This looks like a disagreement about fairness, but B's own conditional ("If it really had used most of the bill, I would agree to pay more") accepts splitting by use when usage differs a lot. B's case for an even split rests on the claim that usage barely differs, not on a different idea of what is fair.
- **Simplicity.** B mentions that an even split is simpler, but B is willing to give that up if the kiln figures go the other way, so it doesn't decide anything here.

### Confidence
High: both conclusions turn openly on the kiln's share of the bill, B says outright that they would change their view if that share were large, and the share can be measured.
