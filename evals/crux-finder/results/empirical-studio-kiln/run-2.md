## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** The bill should follow usage. B fires the kiln most weeks and A about once a fortnight, and the kiln is the biggest load on the meter. So most of last quarter's electricity went on B's firings, and an even split has A paying for them.

**B, strongest form:** An even split is simpler and fair, because most of the bill is heaters and lights, which both use equally. The kiln is well insulated and runs only a few hours per firing, so it was a small share of last quarter's electricity. Had it been most, B would pay more.

### Common ground
- B fires the kiln more often than A [inferred: B does not dispute it].
- If one person's use made up most of the bill, that person should pay more.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the kiln made up most of last quarter's electricity use, or only a small share of it.

- A holds: The kiln is by far the largest load on the meter, so B's frequent firings made up most of last quarter's use.
- B holds: The kiln is well insulated and runs only a few hours per firing, so heaters and lights made up most of the bill.
- If A accepted B's view here: the gap in kiln use would move little money, and A's case against an even split would lose its basis; if B accepted A's: B has already said they would pay more.
- What would move it: For last quarter, multiply the kiln's rated power by the hours in each firing, taken from the controller log or firing records, and compare that with the total kWh on the bill.
