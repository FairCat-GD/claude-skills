## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** She should stop at the end of this term. Her unhappiness shows up consistently: she dreads Tuesday evenings, argues about practising every night, and told her teacher she hates it. Her progress is real, but an after-school activity should be something a child looks forward to, and this one no longer is.

**B, strongest form:** She should carry on. In a year she has gone from barely reading music to playing whole pieces with both hands, and she passed Grade 2 with a merit. She doesn't enjoy it, but lessons are for learning, she is learning fast, and stopping now would waste that progress [inferred: much of what she has gained would fade if she stopped].

### Common ground
- She has learned a lot and is progressing.
- She doesn't enjoy it and complains about practising.
- The decision is about her good, not the parents' preferences.

### The crux
**Double crux · Value**
> With both parents agreeing that she is progressing and that she doesn't enjoy it, the question is whether her enjoyment or her progress should decide whether lessons continue.

- A holds: An after-school activity a child doesn't look forward to should stop, even if she is learning from it.
- B holds: Lessons are there for learning, so fast progress justifies carrying on even though she doesn't enjoy it.
- If A accepted B's view here: A would keep the lessons going despite her dislike; if B accepted A's, B would agree to stop at the end of term.
- What would move it: This is a trade-off between how she feels about her evenings now and the skill she is building. The facts can't settle it, so it's a question of which one the parents weigh more.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Crux for B · Predictive**: Would stopping now mean losing most of what she has gained? A holds: A doesn't address this, and accepts she has learned something either way. B holds: Stopping would throw that away. If accepted: B's case would weaken if her skills would mostly last after a break, and A's conclusion would stand either way. What would move it: Agree now to stop at the end of term, then check in six months whether she can still read music and play her Grade 2 pieces with both hands.

### Not actually in dispute
- Whether she is making progress. Both say she is.
- Whether she enjoys piano. B concedes she doesn't.
- Whether she dislikes the lessons or only the practising. A cites both; B mentions the practising and concedes she doesn't enjoy it overall. Neither parent's conclusion turns on which it is.

### Confidence
High: both parents openly accept each other's facts, so what's left is how much each one counts.
