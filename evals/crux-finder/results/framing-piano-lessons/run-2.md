## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** Her lessons have taught her real skills, but she now dreads Tuesday evenings, argues about practising nightly, and told her teacher she hates it. After-school activities should be something a child looks forward to, and this one is not, so she should stop at the end of term.
**B, strongest form:** In a year she has gone from barely reading music to playing whole pieces with both hands, and earned a merit at Grade 2. She does not enjoy practising, but lessons exist for learning, she is learning fast, and stopping now would waste that progress.

### Common ground
- She has learned a lot from the lessons and is still progressing.
- She does not enjoy the lessons or practising.

### The crux
**Double crux · Value**
> Both parents agree she is learning and does not enjoy it, so the question is whether keeping on with lessons should turn on her enjoyment or on her progress.

- A holds: an after-school activity a child does not look forward to should stop, whatever she is learning from it.
- B holds: lessons are for learning, so fast progress justifies carrying on even though she does not enjoy them.
- If A accepted B's view here: A would conclude she should carry on; if B accepted A's, B would conclude she should stop at the end of term.
- What would move it: the trade-off is how much she enjoys her evenings now against the skill she is building and could lose. No resolution is offered.
