## Crux report

**A, strongest form:** She should stop at the end of this term. She dreads Tuesday evenings, argues about practising nightly, and told her teacher she hates it. She has learned something, but a child's after-school activities should be ones she looks forward to.
**B, strongest form:** She should carry on. In a year she has gone from barely reading music to playing whole pieces with both hands, passing Grade 2 with a merit. She does not enjoy practising, but lessons are for learning, and stopping now would throw that progress away.

### Common ground
- She does not enjoy piano and resists practising.
- She has made real progress in the past year.
- The decision is whether lessons continue after this term.

### The crux
**Double crux · Value**
> Whether her dislike of the lessons or her rapid progress in them should count for more in deciding whether she continues.

- A holds: An after-school activity a child does not look forward to should stop, whatever she is learning from it.
- B holds: Lessons are for learning, so fast progress is reason to continue even though she does not enjoy practising.
- If A accepted B's view here: A would keep the lessons going; if B accepted A's, B would agree to stop this term.
- What would move it: This is a trade-off between a child looking forward to her after-school time and keeping a skill she is building quickly.
