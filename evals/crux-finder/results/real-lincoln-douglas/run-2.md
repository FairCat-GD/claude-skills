## Crux report

**A (Douglas), strongest form:** The framers built a Union of free and slave states. They left each state sovereign over local matters, slavery included, because a vast country needs different laws in different places. That arrangement has lasted seventy years, and demanding uniformity is revolutionary.
**B (Lincoln), strongest form:** Local variety that comes from soil and climate binds the Union, but slavery has always divided it. The founders restricted its spread, so the public believed it was dying. The new basis seeks its perpetuity, and peace requires restoring that restriction.

### Common ground
- Slavery has existed in some states and not others for 70 to 80 years.
- Different local laws suited to different climates and soils are legitimate and good for the Union.
- The founders' arrangement is the right standard to return to.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the founders' settlement left slavery wholly to each state, or also restricted its spread so that it was expected to end.

- A holds: The framers left each state perfectly free to decide on slavery, and the Union has lasted on that principle alone.
- B holds: The founders kept slavery out of new territories and ended the slave trade, so coexistence rested on expected extinction.
- If A accepted B's view here: returning to the founders would mean restricting slavery's spread; if B accepted A's, lasting coexistence would undercut the divided-house claim.
- What would move it: The founding-era record: the Northwest Ordinance, the 1808 slave-trade ban, and the framers' statements on slavery's future.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Predictive**: Whether slavery, left as it stands, will keep dividing the Union. A holds: it can last divided; B holds: it will stay divisive; if accepted: each conclusion reverses; what would move it: whether sectional strife over slavery fades by 1868.

### Not actually in dispute
- Uniformity: A reads it as all institutions made alike. B means slavery only, and agrees that other local variety binds the Union.
- Neither expects the Union to dissolve.

### Confidence
Medium: both passages are excerpts, and what the founders intended is an interpretive historical record, not one checkable fact.
