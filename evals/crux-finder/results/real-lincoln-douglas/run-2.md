## Crux report

**A (Douglas), strongest form:** The framers built a Union of free and slave states, leaving each state sovereign over local institutions, slavery included, since varied climates and interests need varied laws. It has endured seventy years so. Demanding uniformity is revolutionary; in 1787 it would have nationalized slavery.
**B (Lincoln), strongest form:** Local varieties suited to soil and climate bind the Union, but slavery has always divided it. The fathers restricted its spread and ended the slave trade, so the public believed it headed for extinction. Its new basis aims at perpetuity; peace requires arresting its spread.

### Common ground
- The Union has lasted seventy to eighty years with slavery in some states and not others.
- Local laws that vary with soil and climate are legitimate.
- The founders' arrangement on slavery is the model to follow.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the founders left slavery wholly to each state, or restricted its spread so that it was expected to die out.

- A holds: The founders left each state free to decide on slavery, a reserved right like other local matters.
- B holds: The founders kept slavery out of new territories and cut off the slave trade, setting it on course toward extinction.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's appeal to the founders would favour restricting slavery's spread; if B accepted A's, B's new-basis charge would lose its historical ground.
- What would move it: The founding-era record, including the Northwest Ordinance, the 1808 slave-trade ban, and the framers' recorded statements on slavery's future.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Empirical**: Whether slavery has worked like other local laws or as a lasting source of sectional conflict. A holds: a local matter; B holds: always divisive; if accepted: A's permanence claim or B's house-divided claim falls; what would move it: the record of sectional crises since 1787.

### Not actually in dispute
- Uniformity of local laws in general: both accept varied institutions. They disagree only on whether slavery is one of them.

### Confidence
Medium: both passages address the point directly, but the founding record can be read more than one way and may not settle it alone.
