## Crux report

**A (Douglas), strongest form:** The Union can endure permanently half slave and half free, as the framers made it. They left each State sovereign over local institutions, slavery included, because a vast, varied country needs different laws. Demanding nationwide uniformity on slavery is revolutionary and destructive.

**B (Lincoln), strongest form:** Local variety binds the Union, but slavery has always divided it. The founders kept the peace by restricting slavery's spread and ending the slave trade, which put it on a course toward ultimate extinction. Now that slavery is being set toward perpetuity, peace requires stopping its spread.

### Common ground
- Slavery has existed for decades in some States and not in others.
- Differences of soil and climate rightly produce different local laws.
- The founders' arrangement is the right model to return to.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the long coexistence of free and slave States rested on leaving slavery to each State, or on the founders restricting its spread.

- A holds: The framers left each State wholly free on slavery, and that principle alone sustained the Union for some seventy years.
- B holds: The founders kept slavery out of new Territories and cut off the slave trade, so the public believed it was ending.
- If A accepted B's view here: returning to the founders' principles would mean restricting slavery's spread; if B accepted A's, the founders' example would not support stopping it.
- What would move it: The founders' own acts and records, such as territorial laws, the slave-trade ban, and their recorded statements about slavery's future.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Empirical**: Whether slavery is one local difference among others or a lasting source of division. A holds: it is a local matter; B holds: it has always divided; if accepted: each conclusion falls; what would move it: the record of sectional crises since 1787.

### Not actually in dispute
- Uniformity in all local institutions: B rejects it as A does. B's claim of dead uniformity concerns slavery alone.

### Confidence
Medium: these are excerpts, and B's conclusion also rests on slavery's divisiveness, so the founders' record alone may not settle it.
