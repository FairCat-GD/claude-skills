## Crux report

**A (Douglas), strongest form:** The Union can endure half slave and half free indefinitely, as the founders built it: each state decides slavery for itself, like other local laws suited to its climate and interests. Demanding uniformity is revolutionary, and in 1787 would have nationalized slavery.

**B (Lincoln), strongest form:** Local variety binds the Union, but slavery has always divided it. The founders contained it, barring it from territories and ending the slave trade, so the public believed it dying. A recent new basis aims at perpetuity; peace requires containment again, or nationalization.

### Common ground
- The Union has lasted seventy to eighty years with both free and slave states.
- Local institutions rightly differ with soil, climate and interest.
- The founders' arrangement is the right model to return to.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether the Union's long endurance with free and slave states rested on leaving slavery to each state, or on restricting it and expecting it to end.

- A holds: The founders left each state perfectly free on slavery, and the Union can keep enduring on that same principle.
- B holds: The founders restricted slavery's spread and cut off the slave trade, so the public believed it was ending, which kept peace.
- If A accepted B's view here: the founders' precedent would stop supporting indefinite division; if B accepted A's, the divided-house warning would lose its footing.
- What would move it: Founding-era records on slavery's expected future, such as the Northwest Ordinance and slave-trade debates, and how earlier sectional crises ended.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Predictive**: Whether slavery, left as now, keeps dividing the Union unlike other local differences. A holds: it is local like others; B holds: it stays divisive; if accepted: each conclusion flips; what would move it: whether slavery still splits national parties by 1868.

### Not actually in dispute
- Uniformity: A attacks uniformity of all institutions, while B praises local variety and would let slavery alone where it exists. B means uniformity on slavery only.

### Confidence
Medium: both passages engage each other directly, but the founders' intent and what caused past peace are partly matters of historical interpretation.
