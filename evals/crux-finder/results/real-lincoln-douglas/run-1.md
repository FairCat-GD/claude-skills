## Crux report

**A (Douglas), strongest form:** The house-divided doctrine is revolutionary. The founders built a Union of free and slave states, leaving each state to regulate slavery as a local matter suited to its climate and interests. It has lasted that way for seventy years and can continue.

**B (Lincoln), strongest form:** Local variety binds the Union, but slavery has always divided it. The founders restricted its spread and ended the slave trade, so the public believed it was headed for extinction. Recent policy aims at its perpetuity, and peace requires restoring that containment.

### Common ground
- Different local laws suited to soil, climate and interests are legitimate and hold the Union together.
- Slavery has existed in some states and not others for decades.
- The founders' arrangement is the right standard to return to.

### The crux
**Double crux · Empirical**
> Whether slavery, unlike other differences in local laws, has been a lasting source of division between the sections throughout the government's history.

- A holds: Slavery is one local institution among many, rightly left to each state, and the divided Union has lasted seventy years.
- B holds: Slavery has always divided the Union rather than bound it, and it will keep doing so while its position stays unchanged.
- If A accepted B's view here: A's case that the arrangement can last loses its footing; if B accepted A's, the house-divided argument collapses.
- What would move it: Political records since 1787 comparing how often slavery, versus other differing state laws, produced sectional crises that threatened the Union.

### Secondary cruxes
- **Double crux · Empirical**: Whether the founders left slavery wholly to the states or also restricted its spread. A holds: each state was left free; B holds: territories were closed and the trade cut; if accepted: each loses its founding precedent; what would move it: Northwest Ordinance and slave-trade-ban records.

### Not actually in dispute
- Uniformity: A reads it as identical institutions in every state. B means uniformity only on slavery's status, and he praises variety in everything else.

### Confidence
Medium: both sides speak directly to the crux, but Lincoln's appeal to men's moral constitution suggests a deeper value disagreement that these passages leave unstated.
